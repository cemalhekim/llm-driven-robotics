
\begin{abstract}
This thesis investigates the integration of large language models into robotic control systems for autonomous task planning and execution. A locally deployed LLM serves as the reasoning core, translating natural-language instructions into structured commands executed by a robotic manipulator and analytical instruments within a self-driving laboratory. The proposed architecture establishes a direct semantic interface between human intent and robotic action, eliminating conventional programming dependencies. The framework was implemented and validated through simulated and real experiments, demonstrating consistent interpretation, deterministic execution, and low-latency operation. The results confirm that language-based reasoning can provide a scalable foundation for adaptive, human-interpretable, and domain-independent robotic autonomy.

{\bfseries Key words:} LLM-Guided Robotic Control, Human-Robot Interaction, Collaborative Autonomous Environments, Natural-Language-Driven Automation, Self-Driving Laboratory, AI Agents in Robotics and Automation, Task Planning and Execution, Local LLM Deployment, Agentic Control Framework


\end{abstract}
